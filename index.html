About

I study ultrafast light–matter interaction using soft X-ray free-electron lasers, with an emphasis on nonlinear spectroscopy, element-resolved dynamics, and emergent phenomena in magnetic and semiconductor systems.

I am a PhD student in Prof. Matsuda’s group at the University of Tokyo. My research focuses on nonlinear X-ray optics, ultrafast spectroscopy, and free-electron-laser-based probes of matter.

My current work includes soft X-ray second-harmonic generation (SXSHG), time-resolved XMOKE, and element-specific studies of ultrafast dynamics in semiconductors and magnetic heterostructures.

Research Focus

Soft X-ray Second-Harmonic Generation (SXSHG)

Exploring nonlinear optical processes in the soft X-ray regime with elemental selectivity and interfacial sensitivity.

Ultrafast Magnetization Dynamics

Using XMOKE and FEL-based pump–probe approaches to resolve transient magnetic responses in quantum wells and heterostructures.

Nonlinear Optics in Semiconductors

Studying rotational anisotropy, band-structure effects, and symmetry-dependent responses in systems such as GaAs.

FEL-Based Spectroscopy

Developing ultrafast soft X-ray techniques at SACLA and related facilities for next-generation spectroscopy.

About

I am Xing Su, a Ph.D. student in the Matsuda Group at the University of Tokyo, Department of Chemistry, School of Science.

My research sits at the intersection of ultrafast light-matter interaction, nonlinear optics, and soft X-ray spectroscopy using X-ray free-electron lasers (XFELs). I am particularly interested in probing element-specific ultrafast dynamics in magnetic and semiconductor systems, from coherent orbital and spin currents to symmetry-breaking responses that only soft X-ray second-harmonic generation can resolve.

I conduct experiments primarily at SACLA BL1 and am broadly interested in how coherent, element-selective nonlinear probes can uncover the microscopic mechanisms behind spin-orbit and magneto-optical phenomena.

Research

Soft X-ray Second-Harmonic Generation in GaAs

Rotational-anisotropy SXSHG as an element-resolved probe of symmetry and bonding in non-centrosymmetric semiconductors. Measurements performed at SACLA BL1.

Ultrafast Magnetization Dynamics in (In,Fe)As

Element-specific time-resolved XMOKE on dilute magnetic semiconductor quantum wells, resolving the interplay between carrier dynamics and ferromagnetic ordering.

Orbitronics in CoGd / Ti Heterostructures

Using soft X-ray SHG and XMCD to probe element-specific orbital angular momentum transfer across magnetic / light-metal interfaces on femtosecond timescales.
